My Roadmap for learning Go

September 20, 2026

So I’ve tried out a bunch of different programming languages since my interest in programming sparked, and I couldn’t really tell which I liked the most. I really liked the simplicity of Python, but hated its syntax. I really liked the syntax of C/C++ and its low-level power, but hated the linking & CMake. I mean, I have tried Go and liked it very much, but just stopped with Go for no exact reason…

So I wanna learn Go again!#

Well, I really like to plan out things for my own style and make an overview, so I made a roadmap which you might get inspired by and go try out… or even better, make your own roadmap.

Disclaimer: I will mostly just take books as learning “blocks”, because I think they help one the most in building up deep knowledge, and I love books.

The Roadmap#

The foundation#

Foundational Go knowledge Additional Projects
  • I will use “The Go Programming Language” book as my semi-beginner-advanced book to go over syntax and basic Go stuff.
  • Additionally, I will use “Learn Go with Pocket-Sized Projects” as an extra, more hands-on approach to learning various topics about Go and the Go-way to solve problems.

Concurrency#

  • Go is really famous for its concurrency regarding backend/cloud applications, so learning about Go’s particular strength is, in my opinion, a must-have.
  • So therefore, I want to learn more about concurrent programming itself, and especially with Go.

Network Programming#

Professional Cooler Cover (probably my choice xd)
  • Since I have a big interest in cybersecurity, and therefore also networking, I want to get to know more about networking and also how to do it—you guessed it—with GO!

Own interest#

  • Yeah, so hacker gopher = cool
  • People say it’s neither a good Go book nor a “hacker” book, but I’m really curious.
